
Tau independence check: per-SKU correlation between IoU and optimistic error
==============================================================================
  checkpoint        : recog/checkpoints/seg/best.pt
  config            : configs/segmentation.yaml
  synth config      : recog/dataset3d_seg/manifest.json
  population        : cartridges with a PREDICTED bay region (P_direct
                       non-empty) - the same 35 of 126 val crops as
                       docs/receipts/tau_calibration.txt.
  method            : ad hoc analysis script, not part of the committed
                       CLI surface; reads the committed checkpoint and
                       dataset labels only. No retrain, no dataset
                       regeneration, no other receipt touched.

Question: a confidence gate needs IoU and error to be NEGATIVELY
correlated - higher IoU should mean smaller error. Measured here per SKU,
against both the production formulation of P_derived (WITH the
electronics/obstruction/battery subtraction - "OLD", what
plan/arbitration.py actually ships) and an alternative that deletes the
subtraction ("NEW" / a-lenient), since the subtraction is a proven no-op
on P_safe's content under an argmax label map (plan/arbitration.py:
derived_placement; recog/bay_segmenter.py:110 emits
logits.argmax(dim=1)). Deleting it changes only the IoU denominator, not
P_safe.

P_safe content invariance was asserted programmatically and held for all
35 records: OLD and NEW arbitration give BYTE-IDENTICAL P_safe, hence
identical optimistic_error_px and admits_a_cell - only the IoU (the
candidate confidence signal) moves between the two formulations. This is
the same fact plan/arbitration.py's own comment and
tests/test_arbitration.py:87-100/128-147 already establish algebraically;
this run confirms it empirically against the trained checkpoint's actual
predictions, not just against hand-built fixtures.

--- IoU distribution, OLD vs NEW (n=35) ---
  OLD (current, with subtraction): mean=0.7807 std=0.0817 min=0.5715
                                    max=0.9607 frac>0.95=0.0286
                                    (matches docs/receipts/tau_calibration.txt)
  NEW (a-lenient, no subtraction): mean=0.5357 std=0.1336 min=0.1938
                                    max=0.7782 frac>0.95=0.0000

  Deleting the subtraction widens the spread substantially - std rises
  1.64x (0.0817 -> 0.1336), the observed range widens from [0.57, 0.96]
  to [0.19, 0.78] - but per the invariance above, P_safe and the
  optimistic error it is judged against do not move at all.

Reading: a working confidence gate needs these coefficients negative. Under
the PRODUCTION formulation (OLD), the raw (px) correlation is POSITIVE in
all four SKUs, both Pearson and Spearman - the opposite sign a gate needs.
Normalising by area does not rescue it: AnkerPowerCore10000 and
AnkerPowerCore20100 stay clearly positive under both denominators;
AnkerPowerCore13000 sits at approximately zero, marginally negative
(-0.08, on n=10); AnkerPowerCore26800 stays weakly positive (0.11-0.17).
None crosses into the negative territory a gate needs, and three of four
are unambiguously the wrong sign.

Deleting the subtraction (NEW) does not recover a usable signal either:
three of four SKUs (10000, 13000, 20100) sit at zero-to-positive after
normalising; only AnkerPowerCore26800 (n=9) goes moderately negative
(-0.38 / -0.43) - a single SKU, small n, and inconsistent with the other
three, i.e. noise around zero rather than a recovered signal.

Caveat: n=8-10 crops per SKU (9 for 26800). No single coefficient above
should be read as a precise estimate at this sample size - the load-
bearing evidence is the SIGN pattern holding across all four SKUs under
the production formulation (all four positive on raw px), not any
individual number.
